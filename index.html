Global Competitiveness of U.S. Barley

Where can U.S. barley compete, and what stands in the way? This project compares what it costs to grow and move U.S. barley with the main competing exporters, tracks who buys barley and why, projects brewing-barley demand to 2035, and measures the tariffs U.S. barley and malt face abroad.

Key findings

U.S. barley is a malting crop, sold abroad mostly to Mexico

Headline results from the four analyses. Each is explained, with its sources, in the dashboard.

Market position

0.5%

of world barley grain exports. Most U.S. barley is grown under contract for domestic maltsters.

Main customer

82%

of U.S. barley and malt export value goes to Mexico, mostly as malt shipped by rail.

Cost to grow

$129/t

U.S. operating cost, 2020–2024: the same as Argentina and below Canada and the EU.

Outlook to 2035

33.3 Mt

of world barley for brewing, up from 29.9 Mt. Growth is in Latin America and Africa.

Tariff risk

$17–108M

a year in lost U.S. malt sales if Mexico put a 10-point tariff on U.S. malt.

New markets

$626M

of malt imported each year by sub-Saharan Africa, three-quarters of it from Europe.

Scenario tools

Ask your own questions

The Outlook and Tariffs pages run the project’s models live, so growers and Commissioners can test assumptions directly.

  • Change income growth, drinking habits, age structure or Mexico’s beer exports and see 2035 brewing-barley demand update.
  • Put a tariff on U.S. barley or malt, or on a competitor, in any market and see how U.S. sales and market shares shift.
